THE EDITORIAL STANDARD · EDITION 01

Design with
integrity.

A truly excellent research figure is more than attractive. It tells the truth, makes the claim intelligible and respects the reader's attention.

Scientific fidelityClarityVisual craftPublicationWhole-paper coherence

01 · Scientific fidelity

NON-NEGOTIABLE

Scientific claims come before visual effects. Labels, data encodings, axes and comparisons must agree with the underlying evidence. A diagram may be conceptual — but it must say so.

The hard stop

Unsupported results, manipulated observations or misleading visual comparisons are blocking issues, even if the figure looks beautiful.

02 · Clarity of thought

A reader should know what to see first, how to follow the visual argument, and why each panel is present. Cut redundant prose and repeated panels before shrinking the font.

03 · Considered visual craft

Good typography, meaningful whitespace, deliberate contrast and aligned geometry are not decorative extras: they reduce the work required to understand your results. No single aesthetic style is mandatory.

04 · Publication reality

Judge the finished figure where it appears — in the compiled paper or slide. Inspect labels, vectors, line weights, legends and panel balance at the real target size, not only in a zoomed-in editor.

05 · The paper as a story

A strong manuscript decides which claims deserve visual evidence and prevents multiple figures from doing the same job. Style should connect a paper, not distract from its contribution.
